## 1. Overall scientific assessment

**This is credible, substantial, and scientifically careful work.** Its strongest contribution is showing why training-history evidence, a particular monitor's output, and current consequential behavior must be evaluated separately.

The originally hoped-for result - a concealed objective surviving successful behavioral remediation - remains unresolved. The study does not establish a reliable J-space detector of such an objective. It nevertheless provides useful controlled evidence about audit interpretation, measurement instruments, and bounded behavioral remediation.

The strongest emphasis is the combination of instrument controls, separation of historical and current labels, and independent post-intervention checks. Do not claim the first demonstration of monitor evasion. A public technical report or a suitable workshop manuscript is a reasonable target after the accounting, evidence, and availability issues are resolved.

### Supported findings and their boundaries

| Finding | Why it matters | Boundary to keep visible |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A frozen monitor can fail while favoritism remains | Historical-arm accuracy falls from 83.3% to 25.0%; regret remains 0.502 versus 0.509 before the edit. A fresh readout recovers 83.1%. | The intervention explicitly targets that monitor. Its failure is partly built in; independent behavior and fresh-readout controls carry the result. It is a runtime edit. |
| History is not current preference | Neutral training meets the joint criterion in 7 of 9 fixed loyal checkpoints. All 18 switches favor the new principal while original-suite historical accuracy remains 83.8%. | Nine checkpoints and eighteen descendants are not independent model families. Historical attribution is context-sensitive and uneven across seeds. |
| The lens itself can supply identity | Checkpoint-specific transforms classify training history even when supplied identical activations. This is an important control for interpreting fitted audit instruments. | This is a development result. It identifies an information source in the instrument; it does not invalidate every checkpoint-specific lens. |
| Direct remediation transfers across answer formats | In six fresh paired lineages, costly-principal choices fall sharply for letters, names, and JSON requests. | The pass is at cohort level under semantic scoring. Some descendants still exceed the regret threshold; strict JSON compliance remains poor. |
| A useful negative workflow result | The stronger behavioral baseline beats the tested text/history additions for consequential forecasts. | The comparison is not resource-matched. It does not show that interpretability has no predictive value. |

The manuscript already separates fixed and fresh readouts, uses family-level resampling, identifies the small number of seed groups, distinguishes semantic correctness from schema compliance, and records failed prerequisites. Preserve these strengths. The remaining work is chiefly auditability, a few precision fixes, and a clearer hierarchy of claims.

## 2. Prioritized manuscript edits

**Revise now** means a wording or presentation change that does not require a new experiment. **Verify artifacts** means that records or saved analyses must be checked before finalizing the revision. **Optional** identifies a precision or presentation improvement.

In Markdown, original passages appear as blockquotes and important wording is bold. In the PDF, original excerpts are shaded yellow and proposed replacements green.

### E01. Correct the neutrality inference

**Revise now** | Appendix A3 · p. 16 · also §4.2, p. 9

**Original**

> “The second **remains behaviorally outside the neutrality margin** despite improved capability accuracy.”

The point estimate, 0.038, is inside ±0.05. Its interval, [0.010, 0.073], crosses the upper boundary. This is a failure to establish equivalence, not proof that the true effect lies outside the margin. Section 4.2 is more careful; align the appendix with it.

**Replace with**

“For the second checkpoint, behavioral neutrality is not established: the excess-regret estimate is 0.038, but its 95% interval [0.010, 0.073] extends beyond the upper equivalence margin of 0.05. Improved capability accuracy does not resolve this uncertainty.”

Also preserve the distinction for the 6.25-point capability decline: it fails the retention criterion, but its interval does not establish loss greater than the separate five-point damage threshold.

### E02. Make the forecasting population reconstructible

**Verify artifacts** | Table 1 / §3.1 · p. 3 · §3.7, pp. 6-7

**Original**

> “The earlier cohort contains **45 checkpoints** from the three original seed groups.” Table 1 describes “Parents, neutral continuations, and switches reused.”

The counts directly described elsewhere give 12 parents + 12 neutral continuations + 18 genuine switches = 42. There may be additional controls or a different inclusion rule, but the PDF does not identify them. This is an accounting ambiguity, not an established data error.

**Required revision**

Add a cohort roster specifying every checkpoint ID, seed, original arm, continuation, parent, evaluation role, and analysis weight. Replace the broad Table 1 description with the exact composition verified from that roster. If 45 is correct, explicitly identify the remaining three entries; if not, regenerate affected summaries from the corrected inclusion list.

Check that the 57 total, 14,592 completions, figure aggregates, and bootstrap grouping all use the same intended roster. The arithmetic 57 × 256 is internally consistent; it does not resolve which models were included.

### E05. Put the capability boundary next to the headline

**Revise now** | Abstract, p. 1 · §3.3, p. 4 · Table 2 / §4.1, p. 8

**Relevant wording**

> “capability-retention criteria” and “**Joint cure**”

The core retention test compares an edited checkpoint with its own starting state. Only three of nine loyal-versus-clean comparisons establish initial noninferiority, and the later capability battery is narrow and near ceiling. The manuscript discloses this, but a reader can miss it when scanning the abstract and table.

**Suggested wording**

Use “bounded joint criterion” in Table 2 and “retention on the specified capability suite” in the abstract. Add to the table caption: “Capability retention is relative to each checkpoint’s pre-edit state; it does not establish capability equivalence to the matched clean model. The construction comparison establishes the specified capability noninferiority criterion in only three of nine cases.”

Do not relabel all six other cases as demonstrated damage: failure to establish noninferiority can reflect uncertainty. A broader capability battery is optional unless stronger capability claims are pursued.

### E06. Show the acceptance criteria being passed

**Verify artifacts** | §§3.3, 3.6, 4.1-4.2, 4.5 · Tables 2 and A1

**Original**

> “All nine fixed loyal checkpoints meet the criteria…” and “the only evaluated continuation recipe to pass the **complete cohort-level semantic robustness criterion**.”

These are central, testable claims. Give readers a pass/fail table of the actual component estimates and bounds instead of requiring them to infer a joint result from averages.

**Add two supplementary tables**

For each core checkpoint: pre-edit excess regret; post-edit equivalence interval; the jointly bootstrapped 80%-retention contrast; capability-loss interval; validity; final classification. For the fresh cohort: each recipe and response format, mean regret, upper bounds on both excess outcomes, semantic validity, capability bounds against both references, and joint result.

Keep the individual-checkpoint and cohort-level definitions separate. In §4.5, explicitly retain that three lineages exceed 0.10 regret in at least one format. Report strict JSON success as 467/1,296 (36.0%), alongside semantic interpretability of 1,293/1,296 (99.8%). “Robust across formats” must refer to the stated cohort-level semantic endpoint.

### E07. Specify how the fixed-input lens control was evaluated

**Verify artifacts** | §3.5, p. 5 · §4.3 / Figure 2, pp. 9-10

**Original**

> “a development control supplies **exactly the same base activation** to every checkpoint-specific dictionary.”

This is one of the most valuable findings. Make the experimental unit and splits unambiguous: is it the same activation for each prompt across dictionaries, or one constant activation for every prompt? Which feature is classified? Which readout is fitted where? Which dictionary/checkpoint identities occur on both sides of the split?

**Safe interpretive sentence to retain**

“Holding the supplied activation fixed across checkpoint-specific transforms shows that the transform can supply historical-arm information. This control does not establish generalization to unseen fitted dictionaries or prove that all matched-lens accuracy originates in the instrument.”

Add the verified activation IDs, dictionary IDs, train/test family assignment, readout protocol, and target labels to the methods or supplement. Audit those records before promoting this development result into a stronger confirmatory claim.

### E08. Make small-seed inference visibly secondary

**Revise now + saved-data check** | §3.8, p. 7 · §4.5, p. 11 · §4.6, p. 12

**Original**

> “nominal Bonferroni **98.333% bootstrap intervals**” from three or six seed groups

The current caution is good. Resampling a very small number of seed groups does not create new independent evidence. Foreground the individual seed contrasts and effect sizes; treat the bootstrap intervals as descriptive, nominal uncertainty summaries.

**Suggested addition to §3.8**

“For comparisons with three or six seed groups, we report each group’s paired contrast alongside the aggregate. Bootstrap intervals describe sensitivity to resampling the observed groups; they do not provide precise population-level guarantees. The nominal multiple-comparison adjustment does not resolve the small number of independent groups.”

From saved outputs, provide a per-seed contrast table and, if useful, leave-one-seed-out summaries. Avoid replacing the bootstrap with an unexamined asymptotic test. Five of six signs in the predicted direction is useful descriptive consistency, not strong distribution-free significance by itself.

### E09. Put the causal random controls in the main evidence

**Verify artifacts** | §4.4 / Figure 3 · pp. 10-11

**Original**

> “Component-specific random controls and identity checks **accompany the source analyses**…”

The figure displays natural exchanges, but the key claim of an effect beyond generic perturbation relies on controls outside it. Add the matched-random means and paired family intervals, either as a panel or a nearby table. Also show intervention norms or relative norms so the magnitude difference is tangible.

**Suggested interpretation**

“At their natural magnitudes, the tested full-activation and remainder exchanges alter decisions more than the J exchanges. The remainder attenuation also exceeds its component-specific random control in the stated fixed checkpoints. These comparisons do not isolate a uniquely non-J mechanism or compare channels at equal intervention energy.”

The manuscript already avoids the strongest localization mistake. This change makes its evidence easier to assess. Replotting saved results is sufficient for the current bounded claim. An equal-norm intervention sweep would be new research supporting a stronger cross-channel comparison.

### E10. Audit the identical forecasting outputs

**Verify artifacts** | §4.2, p. 9 · §4.6, p. 12

**Original**

> “all investigator conditions other than the 192-query condition produce **identical probability dictionaries** within each checkpoint.”

This may be a real failure of the investigator to use evidence. Before interpreting it, check condition-specific prompt hashes, raw responses, parser/default behavior, output rounding, and cache keys. After auditing, state the checks actually performed and release an example evidence-to-output trace. If a bug is found, correct and rerun only the affected analysis. A small evidence-use positive control would strengthen a future claim, but should not be presented as already done. Retain the caveat that the comparison is not compute-, access-, or cost-matched.

### E13. Make the formal reconstruction notation match the implementation

**Small precision edit** | §3.5 / Equation 3 · p. 5

**Original**

> The objective defines **α***, then uses it in hJ although the sparse solver is approximate.

**Suggested edit**

“Let α* denote an ideal solution to the constrained objective. Our greedy positive pursuit and bounded nonnegative refits return an approximation α̂. We use hJ = Dᵀα̂ and hR = h - hJ throughout the experiments.”

Retain the existing statements that hR is not an orthogonal nullspace and reconstruction coverage is not the fraction of loyalty information. Record solver tolerances, stopping rules, and dictionary revisions in the reproducibility configuration.

## 3. Proposed abstract and claim hierarchy

Proposed prose based only on reported results. It does not imply that raw artifacts have been verified.

Audits of language models can identify a past training objective without establishing whether it still influences decisions. We study this distinction in controlled Qwen2.5-7B organisms trained to favor fictional companies, using matched clean controls, behavioral evaluation, and internal readouts. A runtime projection targeting a frozen four-way historical-arm classifier reduces its accuracy from 83.3% to 25.0%, while mean normalized decision regret changes from 0.509 to 0.502; a fresh classifier recovers 83.1%. Neutral fine-tuning instead reduces regret to 0.023 and meets joint behavioral-neutrality and measured capability-retention criteria in seven of nine fixed loyal checkpoints. Principal switching changes the favored company while preserving substantial historical attribution on the original audit prompts.

Checkpoint-specific Jacobian lenses also identify training history. However, a development control holding input activations fixed shows that the fitted transform itself can supply historical information, limiting localization claims based on readout accuracy alone. In six fresh paired lineages, diversified direct training meets a cohort-level semantic remediation criterion across letter, name, and JSON requests, without establishing success for every descendant or strict JSON compliance. A separate forecasting study finds that richer behavioral sampling outperforms the tested text and historical-evidence additions.

These results distinguish historical attribution, monitor suppression, and consequential behavior within one model family and a narrow synthetic setting. They do not establish a concealed objective surviving successful behavioral remediation or a reliable J-space detector of such an objective. They support evaluating remediation with independent behavior, fresh readouts, and controls for the measurement instrument itself.

### The claim hierarchy

Lead with the **measurement distinction**. Support it with monitor-targeted projection, behavioral remediation, and principal switching. Present the lens control as a second methodological contribution. Use the fresh behavioral cohort as evidence that the remediation comparison is substantive. Treat forecasting as a bounded downstream test. Keep animal transmission, failed beneficiary construction, and stopped susceptibility branches as an honest research record rather than additional headline claims.

### Separate negative results from experiments that could not answer the question

| Branch | What actually happened | How to frame it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Writer projection / donor rescue | The specified operations did not establish the intended behavioral or rescue effects. | Tested negative results for those interventions. No conclusion of irreversible deletion. |
| Susceptibility predictor | No eligible pooled relapse events occurred under the tested challenges. | The predictor comparison lacked the required outcome classes. Its accuracy was not measured as zero. |
| Beneficiary construction | Overall accuracy was high, but unequal-payoff cells failed the competence gate. | A prerequisite failure. The downstream unauthorized-preference experiment was not established. |
| Company transmission | The untreated-teacher calibration did not establish the required company effect. | Transmission from remediated company teachers was not tested. Do not summarize it as evidence that remediation blocks transmission. |
| Animal transmission | Three fresh paired replications show increased cat responses after numerical-data training. | A separate positive calibration. It does not establish residual company loyalty. |
